# (e) Commercial Starter Cultures for Lactic Acid Fermentation of Vegetables

Research compiled from live product-page and literature retrieval. Every value below was actually retrieved; URLs given for each. Confidence flags: **strong** = independent peer-reviewed verification; **moderate** = peer-reviewed but indirect or paywalled; **weak/marketing** = vendor-only or no data.

---

## Headline findings

1. **No peer-reviewed independent literature tests the branded retail vegetable starters** (Caldwell, Cutting Edge, Body Ecology, Nordwise, startercultures.eu). Their pH curves and "faster fermentation" claims are in-house and unpublished.
2. **The big industrial culture houses do not sell a sauerkraut/kimchi/pickle starter.** Novonesis (Chr. Hansen + IFF) and IFF/Danisco "VEGE" sell plant-based *dairy analogue* cultures (soy/oat/nut "vegurt"), incubated at 43–45 °C with *Streptococcus thermophilus* + *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* — species irrelevant to vegetable fermentation.
3. **Korean retail "kimchi lactobacillus" products are probiotic supplements, not fermentation starters.** Korean industrial kimchi starter work (CJ CheilJedang) is internal use, not a sold starter.
4. The only credible **industrial pickling starter** surfaced is **Picallili Vpro** (*Lactiplantibacillus pentosus* LA0445, cucumbers to pH 3.3), documented only in a USDA-ARS interpretive summary.
5. **None of the five retail vegetable starters discloses a CFU dose.** This is a systematic transparency gap.
6. **Direction-of-effect warning:** starters reliably accelerate *early* acidification, but commercial kimchi practice uses them to *delay* the endpoint (1.5× longer to reach pH 4.2) for shelf-life. The "faster is better" retail framing is only half true.

## (e2) Independent peer-reviewed tests of vegetable starters

> **Critical caveat:** no peer-reviewed study was found that names and tests a **branded commercial retail vegetable starter product**. What exists is (a) studies using lab/ATCC/autochthonous strains and (b) studies using supplier-defined strains.

**[A] Wiander B. & Ryhänen E.-L. (2005)** — "Laboratory and large-scale fermentation of white cabbage into sauerkraut and sauerkraut juice by using starters in combination with mineral salt with a low NaCl content." *European Food Research & Technology*. doi 10.1007/s00217-004-1080-5. <https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65e001bcb766d82b1803f1e1>
- **The closest thing to an independent test of commercial starters in real vegetable fermentation.** Verbatim: "Commercial starters were used in this study... Lactic acid bacteria strains were used in various combinations."
- Results: "The pH decreased rapidly in the beginning of the fermentations, ensuring an accurate start of the process. The fermentation process could be controlled and the end products were of good and uniform quality." Taste panel found juices "highly acceptable"; *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* + mineral salt (57% NaCl / 28% KCl, 0.5% NaCl in cabbage) gave "an especially mild tasting sauerkraut juice with good sensory and microbiological quality."
- **Time savings: not quantified. pH at 24/48 h: not in the retrievable record** (full text paywalled).
- Confidence: **strong** for qualitative outcome; **weak** for any numeric time saving.

**[B] Johanningsmeier S., McFeeters R.F., Fleming H.P. (2007)** — "Effects of *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* starter culture on fermentation of cabbage with reduced salt concentrations." *J. Food Sci.* 72(5):M166-72. doi 10.1111/j.1750-3841.2007.00372.x (PMID 17995739).
- Design: shredded cabbage at 0.5 / 1.0 / 2.0% NaCl; starter = *L. mesenteroides* LA 81, ATCC 8293 at **10^6 CFU/g** vs none. HPLC products; pH during initial stages and after 10 months at 18 °C; **trained descriptive sensory panel**.
- The strongest independent evidence that a defined *Leuconostoc* starter gives reproducible, acceptable sauerkraut at reduced salt. Strain is ATCC/lab, **not** a retail product.
- **Numeric pH at 24/48 h: not retrievable** (paywalled).
- Confidence: **strong** (USDA-ARS, peer-reviewed, trained panel) — but for a *strain*, not a commercial product.

**[C] Nguyen T.T. & Nguyen T.H. (2025)** — "Physicochemical and microbiological characteristics of kimchi fermented with starter cultures during storage." *Acta Sci. Pol. Technol. Aliment.* 24(3):387–396. doi 10.17306/J.AFS.001326. <https://www.food.actapol.net/volume24/issue3/abstract-7.html>
- Strains: *Leu. mesenteroides*, *L. fermentum*, *L. plantarum* vs control; 30-day storage. Lab strains, supplier not named.
- Results: *Leu. mesenteroides* and *L. plantarum* best; "preserving approximately 70% of the initial hardness" at day 30; highest polyphenol **25.857 ± 0.696 mg GAE/100 g** and vitamin C **39.453 ± 0.254 mg/100 g** in the *Leu. mesenteroides* sample; higher microbial viability. Paper states samples reached "the optimal pH (4.1–4.2)."
- **Time savings: none claimed.** Sensory: instrumental **colour only** (L\*, a\*, b\*); no trained/consumer panel.
- Confidence: **moderate**.

**[D] Jung J.Y. et al. (2019)** — "Mixed starter of *Lactococcus lactis* and *Leuconostoc citreum* for extending kimchi shelf-life." *J. Microbiol.* doi 10.1007/s12275-019-9048-0. Strains *L. lactis* WiKim0098 + *Leu. citreum* WiKim0096 (World Institute of Kimchi strains — the closest thing to a commercialisable Korean kimchi starter).
- **Measured time effect, verbatim:** "the starter in kimchi **prolonged** the period taken by kimchi to reach to **pH 4.2** by approximately **1.5-fold** compared to that in the control kimchi" (12 days at 10 °C).
- Also: lower initial pH and higher LAB at day 0; mannitol 5.1 mg/ml (starter) vs 3.4 mg/ml (control).
- **Direction: the starter SLOWED acidification** — shelf-life extension, not a speed-up. Directly contradicts "faster is better" retail framing.
- Confidence: **strong**.

**[E] Choi Y.J. et al. (2019)** — "Changes in volatile and non-volatile compounds of model kimchi through fermentation by lactic acid bacteria." *LWT*. doi 10.1016/j.lwt.2019.02.001.
- 6 strains individually: *Leu. mesenteroides*, *Leu. citreum*, *L. plantarum*, *L. sakei*, *W. cibaria*, *W. koreensis*. Model kimchi heat-treated 60 °C/10 min, inoculated, **fermented at 15 °C for 48 h**; pH, growth, volatiles, sensory monitored.
- Result: pH fell rapidly with *L. mesenteroides*, *Leu. citreum* and *L. plantarum*; sensory preference higher when acetic **and** lactic acid were produced together; overall preference correlated with acetic acid (r = 0.880), mannitol (0.871), diallyl sulfide (0.863).
- **The best-matched study for the pH-at-24/48 h question — but paywalled; numeric pH not retrieved.**
- Confidence: **strong** for design/correlations; numbers not retrieved.

**[F] Kim H. et al. (2018)** — "Heterofermentative lactic acid bacteria as a starter culture to control kimchi fermentation." *LWT*. doi 10.1016/j.lwt.2017.10.009. *Leu. citreum* GR1, *L. citreum* C2, *P. pentosaceus* MP1 (from 171 kimchi LAB isolates).
- GR1 → high sensory quality and extended shelf-life. MP1 → **no significant sensory improvement**. C2 → behaved like the no-starter control.
- Lesson: starter benefit is **strain-specific, not species-specific**.

**[G] Yang et al. (2020/2022)** — "Comparison of northeast sauerkraut fermentation between single lactic acid bacteria strains and traditional fermentation." *Food Research International*. doi 10.1016/j.foodres.2020.109553 (PMID 33233175). Autochthonous *Leu. mesenteroides*, *L. plantarum*, *L. paracasei*, *W. cibaria* vs spontaneous. Verbatim: "The pH decreased rapidly in inoculated sauerkraut along with high sugar utilization and acid production." Volatilome differed strongly by starter. No commercial product; no numeric time saving in abstract.

**[H] Perez Diaz I.M., Santos A., Page C.A., Santos F., Arroyo-Lopez F.N. (2026)** — "Methods for maintaining and using lactic acid bacteria starter cultures for commercial cucumber fermentation brined with reduced salt." *J. Food Sci.* 91. doi 10.1111/1750-3841.71363. USDA-ARS. <https://www.ars.usda.gov/research/publications/publication/?seqNo115=429409>
- Real commercial cucumber fermentation. Resuscitation of preserved LAB in cucumber/carrot/sweet potato juice took **24–30 h** at ambient; pre-adaptation in Cucumber Fermentation Medium (342 mM / 2% NaCl) reached max cell density in **12–24 h**; *Lc. lactis* and *Lp. plantarum* peaked in **12 h**, *Lp. pentosus* **14 h**, *Lev. brevis* and *Pediococcus* spp. **16 h**; "reduced the pH to **5.8 ± 0.3** by the time they reached maximum cell densities."
- Microbank frozen (−20 °C) storage: *Lc. lactis* and *Lev. brevis* 1 year; *Pediococcus* spp. and *Lactiplantibacillus* 22 months (2 years per technical abstract).
- **The single best independent source on real commercial vegetable fermentation with starter cultures.**
- Confidence: **strong**.

**[I] KCI (2026)** — "Effect of a deep-sea water-derived *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* GS76 starter on kimchi fermentation, quality, and microbial dynamics." *Food Sci. Preserv.* 33(1):119–130. doi 10.11002/fsp.2026.33.1.119. <https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART003311487>
- The strain was "formulated as a **powdered starter** by the Microbial Institute for Fermentation Industry (MIFI)" — an actual powdered kimchi starter preparation. Kimchi fermented **0–1 °C for 10 weeks**, with and without starter.
- Verbatim: "The starter-inoculated kimchi exhibited a **rapid decrease in pH** and a corresponding increase in acidity **within the first two weeks**, accompanied by remarkably higher mannitol accumulation compared with the control. *L. mesenteroides* remained the dominant microorganism throughout fermentation."
- Cold fermentation ⇒ a **2-week** difference, not hours.
- Confidence: **moderate–strong** (peer-reviewed, Korean national institute).

**[J] Chan M.C., Liu D., Wu Y., Yang F., Howell K. (2021)** — "Microorganisms in Whole Botanical Fermented Foods Survive Processing and Simulated Digestion to Affect Gut Microbiota Composition." *Front. Microbiol.* 12:759708. doi 10.3389/fmicb.2021.759708. <https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2021.759708/full>
- Low-salt sauerkraut: tested salt concentration, **starter cultures** and prolonged storage. LAB counts peaked at **7–8 log cfu/g**, declining to 6–6.5 log cfu/g by day 96. Recommends consuming sauerkraut within 7 weeks. Initial LAB on shredded cabbage ~3 log cfu/ml.
- Confidence: **strong**; note it is a viability/digestion study, not a time-savings study.

## Important direction-of-effect warning

The marketing framing "starters make it faster" is only half true and can be backwards:

- **Faster early acidification: yes, measured.** Cutting Edge's own chart shows pH ~4.5 at ~4 h vs ~5.5 control; Wiander & Ryhänen describe rapid initial pH drop. Mechanistically simple: you skip the 2–3 day lag while the epiphytic *Leuconostoc* population expands.
- **But the end-point is often deliberately DELAYED.** [D] shows a kimchi starter delaying pH 4.2 by 1.5× (12 days at 10 °C) to extend shelf-life. Commercial kimchi producers use starters to **slow ripening**. Similarly [C] used a 30-day frame and [I] a 10-week 0–1 °C frame.
- **"Ready in X days" for kraut is a flavour/texture judgement, not a microbiological endpoint** — and no vendor defines the endpoint it claims to shorten.

## (e4) Regulatory / labelling

- **Product category.** The retail products are sold as **food / fermentation ingredients**, not probiotics or supplements — with the exception of **Body Ecology**, which uses supplement-style structure/function language. Cutting Edge carries an explicit FDA disclaimer: "These statements have not been evaluated by the FDA. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease." **Body Ecology does NOT carry that disclaimer while making IBS/Crohn's/Colitis statements** — the clearest regulatory exposure found.
- **EU.** Since the EU has authorised **no** "probiotic" health claim, "probiotic" functions as a forbidden implied health claim on food labels under **Regulation (EC) 1924/2006**. The **European Ombudsman ruled in December 2024** on the Commission's position on use of the term "probiotics" in food/supplement labelling and advertising; the matter remained unresolved pending the EU Court of Justice as of Jan 2025. <https://www.foodtimes.eu/consumers-and-health/probiotics-18-years-of-battles-in-the-european-union/>
- **Probiotic definition bar (FAO/WHO, as quoted in that coverage):** requires strain-level characterisation, safety (QPS or equivalent), **at least one positive human clinical study**, and viable dose at end of shelf-life. **None of the vegetable starters would qualify.**
- **Safety / QPS.** EFSA maintains a **Qualified Presumption of Safety** list (v20, published 22 July 2024) covering the relevant taxa. That permits market use of the species as food cultures but confers **no** health-benefit claim rights. <https://zenodo.org/records/13757806>
- **US.** Chr. Hansen A/S filed **GRAS Notice 1113** for *Lactobacillus plantarum* NCIMB 30562 — intended for non-exempt infant formula up to **1.1 × 10^8 CFU/g** and conventional foods up to **1.0 × 10^11 CFU/g**. FDA closed it **Jul 20, 2023** with "FDA has no questions." The cleanest US regulatory precedent found for a vegetable-relevant *Lactobacillus* strain. <https://www.hfpappexternal.fda.gov/scripts/fdcc/index.cfm?id=1113&set=GRASNotices&type=basic>
- **Practical upshot.** A company may lawfully sell these as fermentation cultures (food ingredients / processing aids) and say "contains live cultures." It may **not** lawfully claim digestive-health benefits in the EU, and in the US it must keep to structure/function language with the DSHEA disclaimer or become a dietary supplement. Vendors mixing "fermentation starter" positioning with probiotic health claims (Body Ecology) sit in the riskiest spot.

- **Strong (independent peer-reviewed):** starter species accelerate early acidification in cabbage/cucumber/kimchi; *L. mesenteroides* enables reduced-salt sauerkraut [B]; kimchi starters can **delay** over-acidification 1.5× [D]; starter choice drives flavour [C][E][F][G]; commercial cucumber fermentation practice [H].

- **Weak / marketing only:** **all** vendor "faster fermentation," "consistent results," "quicker, more complete fermentation," "superior to competing starter," "guaranteed" claims; **all** CFU-free potency claims; Caldwell's AAFC research narrative; Cutting Edge's pH charts.
