# Magnesium (Mg²⁺) and Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB): Experimental Data Report

**Evidence rules applied to this report:** every source listed was actually retrieved with a search/fetch tool. Where a full text was blocked, that is stated and only the retrieved abstract/snippet is reported. Nothing is cited that was not retrieved. Numbers are given in the units used by the source, with my own unit conversions clearly marked as *derived*.

---

## 0. Verdict summary for the four questions

| # | Question | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is Mg²⁺ an essential growth requirement for LAB? | **YES in defined media, for the strains tested.** Single-omission experiments show Mg is the *only* essential oligoelement for *Lactobacillus delbrueckii* subsp. *lactis*; MgSO₄ is essential for growth of *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* NCFB 2772 at 0.8 mM. For *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* Mg is a required (but "supplemental") defined-medium nutrient. **Not substantiated for *Lactococcus* or *Weissella*** — no omission study retrieved. |
| 2 | Does Mg supplementation speed acidification / raise LAB counts? | **YES in nutrient-limited model media** (5.7-fold CFU in diluted MRS; faster pH drop in wort; 10.59 log CFU/mL optimum in skim milk). **NO in real vegetable fermentations**: the one real kimchi trial using an MgSO₄-containing salt blend acidified *more slowly*; 30% MgCl₂ substitution in radish paocai gave *comparable* acidification and LAB growth. **No experiment retrieved in which Mg alone was added to a vegetable brine and accelerated acidification.** |
| 3 | Does Mg cause bitterness in fermented vegetables or brines? | **MgCl₂ and MgSO₄ are demonstrably bitter to humans** (TAS2R7 EC₅₀ ≈ 6 mM for MgCl₂; detection at 0.16% MgCl₂ / 0.04% MgSO₄ in 0.8% NaCl; MgCl₂ at 0.5% gave the worst flavour scores in mortadella). **NO experimental evidence found that Mg causes bitterness in fermented vegetables or brines.** The only direct napa-cabbage pickle trials retrieved report MgCl₂ *enhancing* taste/umami rather than bitterness. |
| 4 | Kimchi + magnesium? | **Two real papers.** (a) A kimchi-derived starter (*Latilactobacillus curvatus* WiKim0094): 3.0 mM MgSO₄ optimal, 5.7-fold CFU increase — but in 1/5-strength MRS, not kimchi. (b) An actual kimchi trial (Hahn 2002) using salt replacers containing MgSO₄: slower pH drop, LAB counts not different from NaCl control. Plus two studies on radish **paocai** (kimchi-analogous) with 30% MgCl₂ substitution. **No study found on adding Mg to kimchi brine to accelerate fermentation.** |

### 1.2 Direct omission evidence (the strongest finding)

**CLAIM:** Magnesium is an essential nutrient for *Lactobacillus delbrueckii* subsp. *lactis* in chemically defined medium — it is the only trace element that cannot be omitted.
**NUMBERS:** Not numeric; qualitative single-omission result: "**Magnesium was the only essential oligoelement.**"
**SOURCE:** Hébert EM, Raya RR, de Giori GS (2004). *Current Microbiology* 49(5):341–345. DOI 10.1007/s00284-004-4357-9. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15486708/
**CONFIDENCE: strong** for the strains tested.
**Study-quality note:** Uses the standard **single-omission technique** on a complete CDM (each component omitted in turn) — a rigorous design for nutritional requirements. Model system (defined medium), not real food. n and number of strains not stated in the retrieved abstract; the same abstract reports the lysine requirement was strain-dependent, implying multiple strains were tested.

**CLAIM:** MgSO₄ is essential for growth of *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* NCFB 2772; trace elements (Fe, Co, Zn, B, Mo, Ni, Cu) are not.
**NUMBERS:** CDM contained 0.8 mM MgSO₄·7H₂O. Verbatim result: "Sodium acetate, MgSO₄, and MnCl₂ appeared to be essential for growth." "The strain grew well when the trace elements (FeCl₂, CoCl₂, ZnCl₂, H₃BO₃, Na₂MoO₄, NiCl₂, and CuCl₂) were omitted individually or all at once." Omission of Tween 80 or citric acid decreased growth. Addition of 10 µM CaCl₂ had no effect. In the simplified medium: growth rate 0.18 h⁻¹, final OD₆₀₀ 1.4, 250 mg EPS/L.
**SOURCE:** Grobben GJ et al. (1998). *Applied and Environmental Microbiology* 64(4):1333–1337. DOI 10.1128/aem.64.4.1333-1337.1998. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC106151/
**CONFIDENCE: strong** (converges with Hébert 2004) for this strain.
**Study-quality note:** Single-omission design in a fully defined medium. Single strain. Lab model system. Full text retrieved; figures/tables of the omission experiment itself were not read in numeric detail.

**CLAIM:** For *Leuconostoc mesenteroides*, Mg²⁺ is required for exponential growth in a chemically defined minimal medium, but is classed as a "supplemental" rather than one of the four strictly "essential" nutrients.
**NUMBERS:** Essential nutrients: glutamine, methionine, valine, nicotinic acid. Supplemental: 8 amino acids, 5 vitamins, plus "manganese, magnesium, adenine, uracil, and Tween 80."
**SOURCE:** Kim YJ, Eom HJ, Seo EY, Lee DY, Kim JH, Han NS (2012). *J Microbiol Biotechnol* 22(11):1518–1522. DOI 10.4014/jmb.1205.05053. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23124343/
**CONFIDENCE: moderate** (abstract-level only; exact concentrations and omission-growth curves not retrieved — the full-text PDF was image-only with no text layer).
**Study-quality note:** Single-omission technique; relevant organism for kimchi/sauerkraut (heterofermentative). Model system.

**CLAIM:** Mg²⁺ is one of the required components of a CDM supporting six *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* strains.
**NUMBERS:** Growth rate 0.85 ± 0.10 h⁻¹; cell densities up to 3.5 × 10⁹ CFU/mL; aerobic conditions beneficial; 135 mM potassium phosphate buffer; lactose as carbon source.
**SOURCE:** Foucaud C, Francois A, Richard J (1997). *Applied and Environmental Microbiology* 63(1):301–304. DOI 10.1128/aem.63.1.301-304.1997. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1389108/
**CONFIDENCE: weak-or-contested for the *requirement* claim** (Mg²⁺ is listed as a medium component; the omission result and concentration were not retrievable — the PMC PDF download was blocked by an automated download gate).
**Study-quality note:** Six strains; model system.

### 1.3 A relevant counter-nuance: Mn is often described as the *absolute* mineral requirement

**CLAIM:** Lactobacilli have an absolute **manganese** requirement and no iron requirement.
**NUMBERS:** Qualitative: "These results support the absolute manganese requirement for optimum growth of lactobacilli and the needless incorporation of iron in growth media." Four strains tested (*L. acidophilus*, *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*, *L. plantarum*, *L. pentosus*), aerobic and anaerobic, with and without Mn.
**SOURCE:** Imbert M, Blondeau R (1998). *Current Microbiology* 37(1):64–66. DOI 10.1007/s002849900339. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9625793/
**CONFIDENCE: moderate.**
**Study-quality note:** CDM; no growth stimulation by iron in any condition. Caution: this paper frames Mn (not Mg) as "absolute", so "essential mineral" claims for LAB are element- and strain-specific.

**Minor finding (divalent-cation interchangeability, *Lactococcus*):** For calcium-dependent lactococcal phages of the 936 siphogroup, Ca²⁺ could be substituted by **magnesium or manganese**, indicating these phages can use Mg²⁺ in place of Ca²⁺. Source: Mahony J, Tremblay DM, Labrie SJ, Moineau S, van Sinderen D (2015). *Int J Food Microbiol* 201:47–51. DOI 10.1016/j.ijfoodmicro.2015.02.017. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25744695/ — **note this is about phage infection, not LAB growth**, and cannot be used as evidence for a *Lactococcus* Mg growth requirement.

## 2. Does Mg supplementation speed up acidification or raise LAB counts?

### 2.1 Model / simple media — POSITIVE effects

**FINDING 2.1a (kimchi-derived strain; strongest single-factor Mg effect)**
**CLAIM:** Mg²⁺, Mn²⁺ and Ca²⁺ significantly promote growth of a kimchi starter strain; optimisation gives a 5.7-fold increase in viable cells.
**NUMBERS:**
- Strain: *Latilactobacillus curvatus* WiKim0094 (isolated from kimchi). Inoculum 10⁷ CFU/mL; 30 °C; 24 h; static; 1/5-strength MRS (nutrient-limited).
- Single-factor screen concentrations: MgSO₄ 1, 10, 20 mM; MnSO₄·H₂O 0.1, 1, 5 mM; CaCl₂·2H₂O 0.1, 1, 10 mM; CuSO₄ 0.01, 0.1, 1 mM; ZnSO₄·7H₂O 0.01, 0.1, 1 mM; FeSO₄·7H₂O 0.005, 0.05, 0.5 mM.
- Mg²⁺, Mn²⁺, Ca²⁺ enhanced growth dose-dependently. Ca²⁺ at 10 mM gave a **+189.1% CFU** increase vs untreated control (described as the most pronounced single-factor effect).
- Box–Behnken optimum: **3.0 mM MgSO₄ + 0.1 mM MnSO₄ + 4.4 mM CaCl₂** → **6.1 × 10⁸ CFU/mL**, a **5.7-fold** increase over control (**1.1 × 10⁸ CFU/mL**); predicted 5.9 × 10⁸; validation 104% of predicted.
- ANOVA: MgSO₄ (X1) F = 87.55, p = 0.000 (largest single factor); CaCl₂ (X3) F = 66.62, p = 0.000; MnSO₄ not significant as a linear term (p = 0.666). Significant interactions X1X2 (p = 0.014) and X1X3 (p = 0.006). Model R² = 0.9751.
- Zn²⁺: +6.0 to +27.9%; Fe²⁺: +18.4 to +23.9% — **not statistically significant**. Cu²⁺ inhibitory.
- Transcriptomics: 118 DEGs at 12 h, 402 at 24 h; upregulation of *nrdA*, *nrdB*, *purD*, RNA polymerase, ATP synthase, *dgkA* (lipoteichoic acid synthesis).
**SOURCE:** Kim S, Jeong CH, Kim J-C, Lee M, Jeong S-G, Kim HM, Park HW (2025). *J Microbiol Biotechnol* 35:e2505011. DOI 10.4014/jmb.2505.05011. https://www.jmb.or.kr/journal/view.html?uid=6777&vmd=Full
**CONFIDENCE: strong** for growth promotion in this medium; **moderate** for extrapolation to kimchi.
**Study-quality note:** n = 3 triplicates throughout; rigorous RSM design (Box–Behnken, 15 runs) with validation. **Model system: 1/5-strength MRS broth — NOT kimchi.** Critical caveat: the 5.7-fold gain is from the **Mg+Mn+Ca combination**, not Mg alone; the paper does not report a Mg-alone percentage in the retrieved text (it is in Fig. 1, whose bars I could not read numerically). The paper also states the ion ranges were chosen from "metal ion content in kimchi as reported by [ref 18]" (Hwang et al. 2019, *J Sci Food Agric* 99:6474–6481 — a dietary-intake survey, not a fermentation trial).
**Internal tension in the source:** the abstract and discussion call Ca²⁺ the most pronounced factor, while the ANOVA shows the MgSO₄ term had the larger F value and regression coefficient (2.599 vs 0.910). Both statements are reported here as published.

**FINDING 2.1b (Mg accelerates acidification in brewers' wort)**
**CLAIM:** Mg²⁺ addition to wort makes the pH fall faster, raises lactic acid at 48 h, and shortens the time to complete fermentation — without changing the final lactic acid ceiling.
**NUMBERS:**
- Strain: *Lactobacillus brevis* WLP672 in brewers' wort (12% w/w extract), 50 mL, **n = 3 replicates** per treatment.
- Measured initial Mg²⁺ in wort (Table 1, AAS): REF **115.0 ± 6.0 mg/L**; Mg.1 **173.7 ± 2.0 mg/L**; Mg.2 **242.7 ± 11.6 mg/L**; Zn treatments ≈113–114 mg/L. (*Derived:* 4.73 / 7.15 / 9.99 mM Mg²⁺.)
- Methods text states MgSO₄·8H₂O was added at 60 mg/L and 120 mg/L — **this does not arithmetically match the measured +58.7 and +127.7 mg/L Mg²⁺ increases**; the Table 1 measured values are the reliable numbers. Flagged as an internal inconsistency in the source.
- Lactic acid after 48 h: **0.68 g/L (REF)** vs **0.75–0.82 g/L (Mg-supplemented)**. Maximum LA reached 0.82–0.90 g/L in all samples; the text concludes "the total amount of lactic acid produced by the bacteria was the same in all trials, but supplementation with either magnesium or zinc ions **shortened the time required (48 h vs 72 h)**."
- pH at 72 h: **3.90** (Mg.1) and **3.85** (Mg.2) vs **3.95** (REF). Beer after ethanol fermentation: pH < 3.4 (Mg) vs > 3.4 (REF).
- LAB did not take up or release Mg during 72 h; yeast released Mg (≈+10% in Mg.1, ≈+20% in Mg.2).
- Zn²⁺ addition reduced L-lactic acid and raised pH — authors recommend against Zn in sour beer.
**SOURCE:** Ciosek A, Fulara K, Hrabia O, Satora P, Poreda A (2020). *Biomolecules* 10(12):1599. DOI 10.3390/biom10121599. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761399/
**CONFIDENCE: moderate** (statistically significant but small effects; authors themselves state the differences "may be negligible at industrial production").
**Study-quality note:** n = 3; real fermentation matrix (wort), but wort is a nutrient-rich liquid medium, not a vegetable brine. Basal wort already contained 115 mg/L Mg²⁺ (~4.7 mM), so this is *supplementation above an already adequate level*, not correction of a deficiency.

**FINDING 2.1c (Mg + Mn optimisation in milk raises counts)**
**CLAIM:** MgSO₄ and MnSO₄ jointly optimise growth of *L. rhamnosus* FTDC 8313, and both "played an important role".
**NUMBERS:** Central composite design (α = ±1.414), two factors. Predicted optimum **10.59 log₁₀ CFU/mL** at **0.80 mg/mL MnSO₄ + 1.09 mg/mL MgSO₄** (= 800 and 1090 mg/L). Validation deviation **0.30%** from predicted. Bioactives (hyaluronic acid, diacetyl, peptidoglycan, lipoteichoic acid, organic acids) evaluated at the optimum.
**SOURCE:** Lew LC, Liong MT, Gan CY (2013). *J Appl Microbiol* 114(2):526–535. DOI 10.1111/jam.12044. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23082775/
**CONFIDENCE: moderate.**
**Study-quality note:** RSM with validation, but the abstract does not report a no-Mg control or the CFU at zero Mg, so the *magnitude* attributable to Mg alone cannot be extracted. Model system: reconstituted skimmed milk (real food matrix, nutrient-rich). MgSO₄ hydration form not stated (1.09 g/L ≈ 4.4 mM as heptahydrate or 9.1 mM as anhydrous — *derived*).

**FINDING 2.1d (Mg as a stress protectant, not a growth promoter)**
**CLAIM:** Mg²⁺ at 10–50 mM improves thermotolerance of probiotic lactobacilli.
**NUMBERS:** "Concentrations of Mg²⁺ between 10 and 50 mmol l⁻¹ were found to increase the bacterial survival upon heat challenge. Remarkably, Mg²⁺ addition at **20 mmol l⁻¹ led to a 100-fold higher survival** of *L. rhamnosus* GG upon heat challenge." Mg²⁺ also shortened heat-induced extended lag time. Compared against Zn²⁺ and Na⁺. Three strains: *L. rhamnosus* GG, *L. casei* Zhang, *L. plantarum* P-8.
**SOURCE:** Yang Y, Huang S, Wang J, Jan G, Jeantet R, Chen XD (2017). *Lett Appl Microbiol* 64(4):283–288. DOI 10.1111/lam.12716. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28100014/
**CONFIDENCE: moderate**; authors call it "this preliminary study".
**Study-quality note:** Stress-survival endpoint, not acidification. Relevant if the goal is survival of a starter through processing, not speed of acid production.

**FINDING 2.1e (one source claims Mg can *reduce* LAB growth)**
The sour-beer paper states in its introduction, citing Raczyńska-Cabaj et al. 2004 (in Polish, *Acta Sci Pol Technol Aliment* 3:111–118): "In the case of LAB, the addition of magnesium ions to the culture medium was shown to **reduce** the growth of the bacteria." I did **not** retrieve the Raczyńska-Cabaj paper, so this claim is reported only as a statement made by Ciosek et al. 2020. **CONFIDENCE: weak-or-contested**; it conflicts with the findings in 2.1a–2.1c.

### 2.2 Real vegetable fermentations — NULL or NEGATIVE effects

**FINDING 2.2a (kimchi + MgSO₄-containing salt replacer: SLOWER acidification)**
**CLAIM:** Kimchi made with salt replacers containing NaCl, KCl, MgSO₄ and CaSO₄ acidified **more slowly** than NaCl-only control kimchi, with **no difference in LAB numbers**.
**NUMBERS:** Eleven salt replacers (SR-1 to SR-11) composed of NaCl, KCl, MgSO₄, CaSO₄, lysine or glutamic acid; used at **2.5% salt replacement**; fermentation at **20 °C**. Results: "The pH decrease and acidity increase of low-salt Kimchi were slower than those of control Kimchi with NaCl alone." "However, there was **no difference in the number of lactic bacteria** between low-salt Kimchi and control Kimchi." Total microorganism count lower in SR-8 (lysine) kimchi until day 5; yeast growth slower in SR-8. SR-3 kimchi had the hardest texture. Overall acceptability of SR-1, SR-3 (NaCl + KCl + MgSO₄ + CaSO₄) and SR-10 (+0.2% glutamic acid) was **similar to the control**.
**SOURCE:** Hahn Y-S, Oh J-Y, Kim Y-J (2002). "Characteristics of Low-Salt Kimchi Prepared with Salt Replacement during Fermentation." *Korean Journal of Food Science and Technology* 34(4):647–651. ISSN 0367-6293. https://koreantk.com/ktkp2014/thesis/thesis-view.view?ctrlNo=SPGHB5-2002-v34n4-647&tempLang=en
**CONFIDENCE: moderate** for "no acidification benefit from the MgSO₄-containing blend"; **weak** for attributing the effect to Mg.
**Study-quality note:** Real kimchi, real fermentation, 20 °C time-course, sensory + microbiological + texture endpoints. **Confounded:** the blends vary NaCl, KCl, MgSO₄, CaSO₄ and amino acids simultaneously, and NaCl was reduced — so Mg cannot be isolated. Abstract-level retrieval only (full text not reached).

**FINDING 2.2b (radish paocai + 30% MgCl₂ substitution: comparable acidification)**
**CLAIM:** Substituting 30% of NaCl with MgCl₂ (or KCl, or 15+15%) in radish paocai gave **comparable LAB growth, acidification and overall acceptability** to the NaCl control, but MgCl₂ reduced radish hardness.
**NUMBERS:** Treatments: 30% KCl (K30), **30% MgCl₂ (Mg30)**, 15% KCl + 15% MgCl₂ (KMg15); fermentations with *Levilactobacillus brevis* PL6-1. "All low-sodium groups maintained comparable LAB growth, acidification, and overall acceptability, ensuring microbiological safety and stability." "The 30% MgCl₂ substitution significantly reduced radish hardness." K⁺ promoted mannitol synthesis and influenced citric-acid metabolism; KMg15 increased the fruity compound ethyl propanoate. Transcriptomics: targeted (not widespread) gene-expression changes; upregulation of *pduE*; "Mg²⁺ stimulated broader energy metabolism, increasing metabolic diversity."
**SOURCE:** "Transcriptomic and metabolomic insights into how KCl and MgCl₂ drive flavor diversity in low-sodium radish Paocai fermented by *Levilactobacillus brevis*." *International Journal of Food Microbiology* (2026). DOI 10.1016/j.ijfoodmicro.2026.112012. PMID 42585874. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42585874/
**CONFIDENCE: moderate** (title/abstract retrieved via the Europe PMC REST API; author list not captured in the retrieved record).
**Study-quality note:** Real fermented vegetable (radish paocai — the Chinese analogue of kimchi), multi-omics, ion-specific substitution design. Abstract-level retrieval only; exact MgCl₂ molarity, NaCl baseline, day-by-day pH curves and n were not in the retrieved abstract.

**FINDING 2.2c (sourdough: no clear LAB effect of Mg)**
**CLAIM:** Adding Mg (as chloride) to the water phase of backslopped wheat sourdoughs produced **no clear-cut trend for LAB diversity**.
**NUMBERS:** 10-day laboratory backslopped wheat sourdough productions; minerals added as chloride salts: calcium, **magnesium**, manganese, sodium; mineral levels based on screening of flours/waters from three Belgian bakery companies. Effects found: calcium (positive) and manganese (negative) on **yeast** growth; calcium (negative) on **acetic acid bacteria**. "No clear-cut trend concerning the lactic acid bacteria (LAB) diversity was found." 17 typical LAB sourdough species retrieved, dominated by *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum/paraplantarum*, *Levilactobacillus brevis*, *Limosilactobacillus fermentum*.
**SOURCE:** "Impact of minerals on the microbial species diversity and metabolite dynamics during spontaneous backslopped wheat sourdough productions." *International Journal of Food Microbiology* (2026). DOI 10.1016/j.ijfoodmicro.2026.111747.
**CONFIDENCE: moderate** (abstract-level; author list not captured).
**Study-quality note:** Real food fermentation, culture-dependent + culture-independent microbiology + metabolite target analysis, 10 days. Negative result specifically for LAB; no numeric effect sizes were in the retrieved abstract for Mg.

**FINDING 2.2d (MgCl₂ as a *growth inhibitor*, at 32 mM, for an acetic acid bacterium)**
**CLAIM:** 32 mM MgCl₂ significantly delayed the lag phase of *Komagataeibacter xylinus*, and the inhibition **exceeded that of NaCl and KCl at the same molar concentration**.
**NUMBERS:** 32 mM MgCl₂ (= 3047 mg/L as anhydrous; ≈778 mg/L Mg²⁺ — *derived*) significantly delayed the lag phase. Separately: umami taste (3 mM monosodium glutamate) was **enhanced** by adding **7.4 mM MgCl₂**; these two together increased the taste intensity of 51 mM (0.30%) NaCl; adding 0.05% lactic or acetic acid further increased overall taste intensity when all three coexisted.
**SOURCE:** Kurotobi T, Tayama K. "Fundamental Analysis of New Fermented Pickles Using Bittern (Magnesium Chloride)." Conference paper No. 2343, Society of Sea Water Science, Japan. https://www.saltscience.or.jp/images/2025/03/E_2343.pdf
**CONFIDENCE: weak** for the inhibition claim (conference proceedings abstract, not a full peer-reviewed paper; single organism; no n, replicates or statistics given in the retrieved text; the host society promotes salt/bittern use — potential interest bias).
**Study-quality note:** Note this is the *opposite* direction to a growth-promotion claim, and it is the only retrieved source giving a specific inhibitory Mg²⁺ concentration in a fermentation context. **Not a LAB** — *Komagataeibacter xylinus* is an acetic acid bacterium.

**FINDING 2.2e (the same group's full paper: MgCl₂ used in a napa-cabbage LAB pickle)**
**CLAIM:** Bittern (MgCl₂) plus a small amount of agar added to lactic-fermented Chinese cabbage improved overall taste and sourness; LAB grew to > 3 × 10⁸ CFU/mL.
**NUMBERS:** Vegetables soaked in **5% NaCl** (2× vegetable weight) for 3 days, then fermented at **30 °C for 4 days** in the main pickling solution (containing MgCl₂ + LAB). LAB growth **2 × 10⁷ – 3 × 10⁸ CFU/mL** (conference abstract) / **> 3 × 10⁸ CFU/mL** (2025 paper). Sodium ion in vegetables **1.0–1.3 × 10⁴ ppm**. End of pickling: **pH 3.4–4.4** (conference) and **~3.5** (2025 paper); salt equivalent **3–4% w/v** (conference) and **~2.5% NaCl** (2025 paper); total L-lactic + acetic acid **0.3–0.4% w/v**, with **L-lactic acid ~0.43%** (2025 paper); glutamate 0.1–0.3%; **MgCl₂·6H₂O added at 0.75%** (≈7.5 g/L ≈ 36.9 mM — *derived*). Vegetables heated at 65 °C for 20 min as a germ countermeasure; commercial LAB drink used as inoculum source; consommé powder as nutrient source; a small amount of vinegar for preservation. Student evaluation: "fully edible". Agar at 0.2% enhanced sourness.
**SOURCES:**
- Kurotobi T, Tayama K (2025). "Quickly production method for making lactic acid fermented pickles using Chinese cabbage by addition of magnesium chloride and agar." *Journal for the Integrated Study of Dietary Habits* 36(3):163–173. DOI 10.2740/jisdh.36.3_163. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jisdh/36/3/36_163/_article/-char/en
- Kurotobi T, Tayama K. Conference paper No. 2343, Society of Sea Water Science, Japan. https://www.saltscience.or.jp/images/2025/03/E_2343.pdf
**CONFIDENCE: moderate** for "MgCl₂ can be used in a LAB vegetable pickle at ~0.75% without preventing fermentation"; **weak** for any claim that Mg *accelerates* fermentation (no Mg-vs-no-Mg acidification-rate comparison was reported in either retrieved text).
**Study-quality note:** Real vegetable (Chinese cabbage = the kimchi cabbage species), real LAB fermentation, pH/lactic acid/CFU endpoints. **But: no controlled Mg-only comparison; no n or statistics in the retrieved abstracts; the 2025 paper is a "Brief Report"; the conference abstract is not peer-reviewed.** Bittern was added for *taste*, not for fermentation acceleration.

### 2.3 Industrial lactic acid fermentation (non-food, for completeness)

**CLAIM:** Mg salts are used at industrial scale as the neutralizing base in lactic-acid fermentation (forming magnesium lactate), enabling very high product titres.
**NUMBERS:** In-situ product removal by crystallization at 42 °C: product concentration **143 g/L**, productivity **2.41 g/L/h**, yield **94.3%**; over four crystallization cycles, average medium reuse 64.0% and product removal 77.7%; ISPR saved 40% water, 41% inorganic salts and 43% yeast extract vs fed-batch.
**SOURCE:** Wang Y, Cai D, Chen C, Wang Z, Qin P, Tan T (2015). *Bioresource Technology* 198:658–663. DOI 10.1016/j.biortech.2015.09.058. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26433791/
**CONFIDENCE: strong** for the process numbers.
**Study-quality note:** Peer-reviewed but **industrial biotechnology, not food fermentation**. The mechanism is pH neutralisation (Mg(OH)₂ slurry), not Mg nutrition. A related patent (WO2017178426A1, Purac Biochem BV — **not peer-reviewed**) claims a process keeping solid magnesium lactate at 5–40 vol.% for ≥40% of operating time; it lists *Lactobacillus*, *Leuconostoc*, *Pediococcus*, *Lactococcus*, *Streptococcus* among suitable lactic acid producers. https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2017178426A1/en

## 3. Does Mg cause bitterness in fermented vegetables or brines?

### 3.1 Human sensory panel data — MgCl₂/MgSO₄ are detected and change taste

**CLAIM:** In a 0.8% NaCl solution, MgSO₄ at 0.04% and MgCl₂ at 0.16% are detectable and change the taste; bittern components reduce saltiness as their quantity increases; taste-sensor responses to MgSO₄ and MgCl₂ show *different bitterness patterns* from other solutions.
**NUMBERS (as reported):** KCl and Na₂SO₄ detected at **0.08%** in 0.8% NaCl. **MgSO₄ at 0.04%** and **MgCl₂ at 0.16%** in 0.8% NaCl "affected the taste". Bittern compounds decreased saltiness in proportion to the amount added.
*Derived conversions:* 0.04% MgSO₄ = 400 mg/L ≈ **1.62 mM** as MgSO₄·7H₂O or ≈3.32 mM as anhydrous. 0.16% MgCl₂ = 1600 mg/L ≈ **7.9 mM** as MgCl₂·6H₂O or ≈16.8 mM as anhydrous. 0.8% NaCl = 137 mM.
**SOURCE:** Endo Y, Ishikawa K (2015). "Effects of Bittern Components on the Taste of Salts." *Bulletin of the Society of Sea Water Science, Japan* 69(2):105–110. DOI 10.11457/swsj.69.105. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/swsj/69/2/69_105/_article/-char/en
**CONFIDENCE: moderate.**
**Study-quality note:** Peer-reviewed, human sensory evaluation **plus** taste-sensor measurement, in a simple salt-solution matrix (not a fermented vegetable). The retrieved abstract does not give panel size, thresholds by forced-choice methodology, or intensity scales. Note the reported detection levels are not labelled explicitly as "bitterness thresholds" in the abstract — "affected the taste" is the wording.

### 3.2 Receptor-level mechanism and potency (concrete mM numbers)

**CLAIM:** The human bitter-taste receptor **TAS2R7** is activated by divalent/trivalent metal salts including MgCl₂, providing a molecular basis for bitterness of Mg salts.
**NUMBERS:** In HEK293 cells expressing human TAS2R7 with Gα16-gust44, screening at 20 mM. **MgCl₂ EC₅₀ = 6.07 ± 1.07 mM** (assay containing 2 mM Ca²⁺) and **5.78 mM** (calcium-free assay). For comparison: CaCl₂ 5.27 ± 0.50 mM; MnCl₂ 6.59 ± 1.73 mM; CuSO₄ 1.04 ± 0.36 mM; ZnSO₄ 33.36 ± 0.14 mM; Al₂(SO₄)₃ 39 ± 15 µM. TAS2R7 did **not** respond to KCl at any concentration tested. Anion identity made little difference (ZnSO₄ 3.21 mM vs ZnCl₂ 3.42 mM).
**SOURCE:** Wang Y, Zajac AL, Lei W, Christensen CM, Margolskee RF, Bouysset C, Golebiowski J, Zhao H, Fiorucci S, Jiang P (2019). "Metal Ions Activate the Human Taste Receptor TAS2R7." *Chemical Senses* 44(5):339–347. DOI 10.1093/chemse/bjz024. PMCID PMC6538953. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6538953/
**CONFIDENCE: strong** for the receptor pharmacology; **moderate** as an explanation of whole-food bitterness.
**Study-quality note:** Experiments replicated 3 times; cell-based assay (HEK293), **not a human sensory panel**. 6.07 mM MgCl₂ ≈ 578 mg/L as anhydrous (≈0.058%) — *derived* — in the same order of magnitude as the human detection level of 0.16% MgCl₂ reported in §3.1.

**CLAIM:** A lipid-membrane taste sensor responds to MgCl₂ at a much lower concentration than to sodium ions.
**NUMBERS:** MgCl₂ threshold concentration on the sensor: **0.01–0.1 mM**, acting most strongly on the "C membrane" among negatively charged membranes; described as **30–100× stronger than sodium ions**; interpreted as complex formation by polyvalent metal ions. Reference solution 1 mM KCl.
**SOURCE:** Iiyama S, Ezaki S, Yahiro M, Toko K (2010). "親水性無機塩の苦味機構" [Bitterness mechanism of hydrophilic inorganic salts]. *Abstracts of the Annual Meeting of the Japanese Society of Cookery Science* 22:20. DOI 10.11402/ajscs.22.0.20.0. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ajscs/22/0/22_0_20/_article/-char/ja/
**CONFIDENCE: weak.**
**Study-quality note:** **Conference abstract, not a full peer-reviewed paper. Artificial taste sensor, NOT a human panel.** The 0.01–0.1 mM figure must not be presented as a human bitterness threshold — it is ~60–600× below the human-relevant range implied by §3.1 and the TAS2R7 EC₅₀. Useful only as a mechanistic/qualitative signal.

### 3.3 Real food matrices

**CLAIM:** In reduced-fat mortadella, the blend giving the best emulsion stability (1% NaCl + 0.5% KCl + 0.5% MgCl₂) gave the **worst flavour scores**.
**NUMBERS:** NaCl replacement of 50–75%; blends of CaCl₂, MgCl₂, KCl. Best sensory acceptance when 50% NaCl was replaced by 25% CaCl₂ + 25% KCl. "The use of a blend with 1% NaCl, 0.5% KCl and 0.5% MgCl₂ resulted in the best emulsion stability, but the worst scores for flavor." CaCl₂ reduced emulsion stability, cooking yield, elasticity and cohesiveness and increased hardness. No effect on colour, appearance or aroma; stable lipid oxidation over shelf life.
**SOURCE:** Horita CN, Morgano MA, Celeghini RMS, Pollonio MAR (2011). *Meat Science* 89(4):426–433. DOI 10.1016/j.meatsci.2011.05.010. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21645975/
**CONFIDENCE: moderate** (0.5% MgCl₂ in a real food produced worst flavour).
**Study-quality note:** Real food, sensory panel, emulsion/physicochemical endpoints. **Not a fermented vegetable.** Abstract-level retrieval; panel size and scale not in the abstract.

**Secondary (review-level) statement:** A US Institute of Medicine report table classifies CaCl₂, MgCl₂ and MgSO₄ as suitable for "few foods", "somewhat salty but with many off-tastes", and states that "bitter tastes of MgSO₄ are usually perceived only at high levels", citing Murphy et al. 1981 (*Physiology & Behavior* 26(6):1083–1095) and Kilcast & den Ridder 2007.
**SOURCE:** Institute of Medicine (2010), *Strategies to Reduce Sodium Intake in the United States*, Appendix D, Table D-1. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK50965/?report=classic
**CONFIDENCE: weak-or-contested** as evidence (this is a **secondary citation**; I did **not** retrieve Murphy et al. 1981 or Kilcast & den Ridder 2007, so their numbers cannot be quoted).

**Counter-evidence in a fermented vegetable:** See §2.2e — in lactic-fermented Chinese cabbage pickles, adding MgCl₂·6H₂O at **0.75%** (≈36.9 mM — *derived*) was reported to **enhance overall taste and sourness**, and 7.4 mM MgCl₂ enhanced the umami of 3 mM MSG; the authors recommend it for elderly people with dulled taste. This directly conflicts with a simple "Mg = bitter defect" narrative at these concentrations.
**CONFIDENCE: weak** (brief report + non-peer-reviewed conference abstract; no trained-panel bitterness scores retrieved; likely interest bias from a salt/bittern society).

**Note on the salt-reduction literature:** A review of salt reduction in vegetable fermentation names **KCl, CaCl₂ and ZnCl₂** as the most promising NaCl replacers and does **not mention magnesium** in its abstract. Source: Bautista-Gallego J, Rantsiou K, Garrido-Fernández A, Cocolin L, Arroyo-López FN (2013). *Journal of Food Science* 78(8). DOI 10.1111/1750-3841.12170.
**CONFIDENCE: moderate** for the observation that Mg salts are not among the favoured replacers in this literature.

### 3.4 Bitterness in fermented vegetables/brines — **NO EXPERIMENTAL EVIDENCE FOUND**

**No sensory panel study, taste threshold determination, or bitterness-intensity measurement for MgCl₂ or MgSO₄ in sauerkraut, kimchi, pickle brine, or any vegetable brine was found.** Searches for "magnesium chloride bitterness sensory", "bitter taste threshold magnesium chloride mM", "bitter salts brine pickle", "sauerkraut brine bitterness minerals magnesium" returned only: (a) simple salt-solution thresholds (§3.1), (b) receptor/sensor work (§3.2), (c) a meat product (§3.3), and (d) non-peer-reviewed/botanical or blog-type pages that cannot be used as evidence.
The only relevant empirical observation in an actual fermented vegetable is the opposite direction of effect (taste enhancement at 0.75% MgCl₂·6H₂O, §2.2e).

## 5. What I could NOT substantiate (explicit list)

1. **No defined-media Mg-requirement (omission) study for *Lactococcus* or *Weissella*** was retrieved. The *Lactococcus* hit obtained concerned phage infection (Ca²⁺ substitutable by Mg²⁺/Mn²⁺), not bacterial growth.
2. **No Mg concentration could be extracted** for the *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* CDMs of Foucaud et al. 1997 or Kim et al. 2012 — PMC's PDF download gate and an image-only (no text layer) PDF blocked extraction. Both confirm Mg is a required medium component qualitatively only.
3. **No human sensory bitterness *threshold* (in mM or %) for MgCl₂ or MgSO₄ in brine, sauerkraut, kimchi, or any fermented vegetable.** The only human data are in a simple 0.8% NaCl solution.
4. **No experimental evidence that Mg causes bitterness in fermented vegetables.** The only direct fermented-vegetable observations report taste enhancement.
5. **MacLeod RA & Snell EE (1947), "Some mineral requirements of the lactic acid bacteria," *J Biol Chem* 170:351–365 (DOI 10.1016/S0021-9258(17)34964-5)** — bibliographic record verified via Crossref, but the **full text was not retrievable** (publisher returned HTTP 403 / bot challenge), so no findings or numbers from this classic paper are reported here.
6. **De Man, Rogosa & Sharpe (1960), "A medium for the cultivation of lactobacilli," *J Appl Bacteriol* 23(1):130–135 (DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2672.1960.tb00188.x)** — bibliographic record verified via Crossref, but the **MgSO₄/MnSO₄ content of MRS could not be verified from a retrieved primary or datasheet source** (Oxoid and Carl Roth product pages were JS-rendered/blocked). No MRS Mg concentration is asserted anywhere in this report.
7. **Hwang et al. 2019 kimchi elemental data (Mg content of kimchi in mg/kg)** — not retrieved (Wiley abstract page and BVS record both blocked).
8. **Zhao et al. 2025, *J Agric Food Res* 24:102383 (MgCl₂ in radish paocai)** — metadata verified via Crossref, **content not retrievable** (ScienceDirect HTTP 403). No numbers reported.
9. **Sauerkraut + Mg:** the only sauerkraut fermentation trial reached (Wiander & Palva 2011, *Agric Food Sci* 20(2):169–174, DOI 10.2137/145960611797215718) uses **mineral salt in which "NaCl is partially replaced by KCl"** per its abstract; the full-text PDF download was blocked by a bot check, so **whether MgSO₄ was also present could not be verified**, and no sauerkraut Mg trial was found.
10. **Murphy et al. 1981 (*Physiol Behav* 26(6):1083–1095)** — cited *by* the IOM report but not retrieved; its halide-salt taste data cannot be quoted.
11. **WHO / Health Canada sulfate taste-threshold documents** (which gave numeric taste thresholds for sulfate salts in search snippets) were **not fetchable** — WHO IRIS returned an HTML gate page, and the Health Canada PDF failed to download (HTTP/2 error, then timeout). Therefore **no MgSO₄ taste-threshold value from those documents is quoted here.**
12. **The 2013 *J Food Sci* review's own experimental content** on vegetable fermentation was only retrieved at abstract level; only its statement about KCl/CaCl₂/ZnCl₂ is reported.
