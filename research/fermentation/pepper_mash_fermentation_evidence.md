# Pepper Mash Fermentation — Cited Evidence Review
**Scope:** fermentation of chili/pepper mash (Tabasco-style, high salt) — temperature optima, duration, salt effects, community, colour/capsaicin.
**Rule applied:** only sources actually retrieved via web_search/web_fetch. Nothing invented. Every number below was read in a retrieved document.

---

## 0. HEADLINE ANSWERS

1. **"Tabasco ferments 3 years in oak barrels at 8–10% salt"** — the *barrels* and the *up to 3 years* ARE company-documented (McIlhenny). The **8–10% salt is NOT stated by McIlhenny** in any retrieved source; the company says only "a small amount of salt". The 8% figure comes from LSU theses on the Louisiana industry. **NO retrievable source — peer-reviewed, company, or patent — states the barrel-warehouse temperature.** See §1 and §6.
2. **High salt does NOT select for LAB in pepper mash.** The two most relevant studies (Li 2020 *Food Microbiology*; Aryee 2022 *Food Chem Adv*) show Enterobacteriaceae persisting/dominating at ≥10–25% salt. LAB dominance is documented at LOW salt (≤5%) and/or with starters. See §4.
3. **Direct temperature comparison exists**: Alberto et al. 2013 ran pepper fermentation at **22 °C vs 30 °C, 4% NaCl** — 30 °C gave faster acidification, 22 °C gave better LAB survival and *more* yeast growth. See §3.
4. **The Frontiers in Microbiology 2022 article (10.3389/fmicb.2022.929738) is about SAUERKRAUT, not pepper.** Lead debunked. See §7.

## 1. TRADITIONAL TABASCO-STYLE PROCESS (company / patent documentation)

### 1a. McIlhenny Company blog, "About Our Barrels", 28 Aug 2019 — COMPANY DOCUMENTATION
URL (retrieved): https://www.tabasco.com/blog/about-our-barrels
Q&A with **Coy Boutte**, barrel-warehouse manager, McIlhenny Company.
Retrieved statements, verbatim/near-verbatim:
- Barrels: "decommissioned white oak bourbon barrels sourced from different distilleries around the country"
- "After red peppers reach the perfect shade of deep red, they are carefully handpicked, mashed, and **mixed with a small amount of salt** on the same day."
- Mash goes to Avery Island, La., "put into white oak barrels in the barrel warehouse. The barrel tops are then sealed with salt to form a natural protective barrier that also allows for the release of gases produced during the slow fermentation process."
- "Pepper mash is aged in these barrels for **up to three years** before it's blended with high-quality distilled vinegar for **28 days**, strained, and bottled."
- Barrel reuse: "typically used 15-20 times on average, which is about 35-40 years"; some "nearly 80-90 years old"
- Scale: "about **73,000 barrels** in the barrel warehouse"; "**20,000 to 22,000 barrels** are put into production each year"
- History: "By 1900, McIlhenny Company had switched to solely using white oak barrels" (previously stoneware jars)
- **NO salt concentration. NO temperature. NO pH/ acidity figures.**

**Confidence: STRONG** for barrel type, duration (≤3 yr), 28-day vinegar blend, scale, history — it is the manufacturer's own published statement.
**Confidence: NONE / NOT VERIFIED** for salt % and for any temperature.

### 1b. McIlhenny Co. product specification sheet — COMPANY DOCUMENTATION
URL (retrieved): https://www.foodingredientsonline.com/doc/pepper-sauce-0001 ("Source: McIlhenny Co.")
- "The red peppers are fermented and aged for **three years**, and then drained to remove excess moisture. This aged pepper mash is blended with vinegar and mixed for 28 days."
- **Finished-sauce** analytical data (NOT mash): pH **2.70–3.10**; Salt **1.40–2.20**; Acid as acetic **8.10–8.90**; Total solids **3.60–5.60**; specific gravity **1.024**; viscosity **400–1600 cP/50°F**; pungency **2500–5000 Scoville**
- **Finished-sauce** microbiological data: Standard plate count **<100 cfu/g**; Yeast **<100 cfu/g**; Mold **<100 cfu/g**; **Lactic Acid Bacteria <10 cfu/g**; Coliform **<0.3 mpn/g**; *E. coli* **<0.3 mpn/g**; Salmonella **negative**
- STORAGE: "Product is to be stored in a cool (**not to exceed 80°F**) dry warehouse or storage area." — **80 °F = 26.7 °C. This is the storage limit for the FINISHED BOTTLED SAUCE, not the fermentation temperature, and must not be quoted as the mash temperature.**

**Confidence: MODERATE** — manufacturer-sourced spec sheet, but undated and third-party-hosted. The LAB <10 cfu/g in finished sauce is a notable endpoint (no live LAB in the bottled product).

### 1c. US Patent 107,701 — Edmund McIlhenny, "Improvement in pepper-sauce", granted 27 Sep 1870 — PRIMARY PATENT
URL (retrieved): https://patents.google.com/patent/US107701A/en
Original process as written:
- Ripe fruit mashed to pulp, mixed with fine vinegar and rock salt "in the proportions of **one pint of vinegar and one handful of salt to every gallon**"
- "The receptacle containing this mixture is closely covered, and the latter macerated for **about six weeks**"
- Pulp worked through a sieve; "**About one drop of bisulphate of lime is then added to every ounce of mixture, for preventing fermentation.**"
- Skins/seeds residue "potted for about twenty-four hours, with an ounce of alcohol to each pound of the residue"; pressed; the two liquids combined and passed through a fine flour sieve.
- Inventor location: New Iberia, Louisiana. Prior art date / publication 1870-09-27.

**Confidence: STRONG** (primary patent text retrieved).
**Significance:** The 1870 patented process was a **~6-week vinegar/salt maceration explicitly intended to PREVENT fermentation** — not a 3-year barrel ferment. The long barrel ferment is a later evolution. Any narrative that "Tabasco has always been fermented 3 years" is contradicted by the company's own founding patent.

### 1d. Trade article confirming the company account (no new primary data)
VinePair, Tim McKirdy, 9 Mar 2020, https://vinepair.com/articles/tabasco-bourbon-barrels/
Re-reports the Tabasco blog: warehouse on Avery Island, casks "stacked six high", "68,000 and 73,000 barrels", "up to three years", salt-sealed tops. **No temperature.**
**Confidence: WEAK** — trade/blog secondary source; used only as corroboration that no temperature is published.

## 2. PEER-REVIEWED / THESIS STUDIES ON CAPSICUM MASH & BRINE

### 2a. Koh, Foong Ming (2005) — LSU MS thesis — THE "8% SALT TABASCO MASH" PRIMARY SOURCE

**⚠ CRITICAL GAP: This thesis states NO fermentation temperature.** A full-text search for temperature terms returned only analytical/laboratory temperatures (oven 60 °C, HPLC 85 °C, water bath 80 °C, storage at −5/0/5 °C) and "ambient temperature" as an **HPLC operating condition** — never a fermentation or warehouse temperature.

**Confidence: STRONG** for 8% salt, the pH/TA/capsaicin numbers and the barrel comparison (full primary text retrieved). Thesis, not a journal article.

### 2b. Awad, Abdul Rahman (1986) — LSU PhD dissertation — SALT × COLOUR × WAREHOUSE TEMPERATURE
"Chemical and Physical Characteristics of Pepper Mash and Hot Pepper Sauce." PhD dissertation, Louisiana State University. DOI **10.31390/gradschool_disstheses.4217**. Landing page: https://repository.lsu.edu/gradschool_disstheses/4217 (full 188-page text retrieved via proxy).

Design: **three salt treatments — 6%, 8% and 10%** — in **55-gallon oak and plastic barrels**, **two years** of aging. Samples dated **12 Oct 1982 → 17 Oct 1984** (14 sampling dates). Analyses: pH, titratable acidity, salt content, colour change (ΔE), total pectic substances.

**★ TEMPERATURE — the closest statement found anywhere:**
> "These barrels were stored in a large warehouse at **ambient temperature**."
No numeric value, no control, no setpoint.

Other retrieved statements and numbers:
- **General industry practice (as stated in its literature review):** "The mash is mixed with **8 to 20 percent salt** and aged. This aging process could be **several weeks to three years** in duration."
- **Salt effect on acid production:** "greater acidity resulted with low concentrations of salt than with high concentrations in both oak and plastic barrels… the level of **6% salt enhanced the activities** of most of the pepper mash microorganisms… the level of **10% salt inhibited most of the microflora except the salt tolerant microorganisms** and enzymes which produced little change in the acidity… the **8% salt concentration produced an intermediate effect**."
- **10% salt lag:** "With high levels of salt, this period of time may be considered as a **lag phase during which the microorganisms adapted to the high level of salt**."
- **★ HIGH TEMPERATURE REDUCES ACIDITY (spoilage signal):** "The acidity was reduced sharply during those hot months even though the concentration of salt remained constant… the **high temperatures during aging encouraged the rapid growth of acid utilizing bacteria and scum yeasts**, thereby causing a sharp reduction in the acidity." "The high temperatures in **June** also caused an unusual sharp increase in the pH value of all samples."
- **Spoilage observed:** "a noticeable **putrefied odor**, especially in the plastic samples" in the last months of aging at some conditions.
- **Colour:** total colour difference (ΔE) was measured at all three salt levels over two years. "The oak barrel hot pepper mash that was treated with **8% salt** was found to contain a higher amount of pectic substances than any other of the treatments and **with less color changes** as compared with the standard sample."
- **Carotenoid/temperature link (from its literature review, citing others):** "Carotenoid components such as **capsanthin** were also affected by the **light and temperature** during the fermentation process of vegetable products" (cites Dougherty & Nelson 1974; MacKinney & Little 1962; Michael & Eskin 1979 — **these three were NOT retrieved**).
- **Pectin:** degraded "very rapidly after only a few days of aging"; retarded by higher salt or by oak barrels; optimum pH for degradation "≤4.20 or ≥4.60".

**Confidence: STRONG** for all retrieved statements (full primary text). The "ambient temperature" statement is a **strong negative finding** — the classic Louisiana pepper-mash process was deliberately un-temperature-controlled.

## 3. TEMPERATURE — DIRECT COMPARISONS AND TEMPERATURE-RELATED OUTCOMES

### 3a. ★ Alberto, Perera & Arena (2013) — the one true 22 °C vs 30 °C head-to-head
"Lactic Acid Fermentation of Peppers." *Food and Nutrition Sciences* **4**(11A):47–55. DOI **10.4236/fns.2013.411A007**. Full text retrieved: https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/455f/63e8f54dbe75e5813e8ccd3a33f816e4390b.pdf

Design: *Capsicum annuum*, grossum variety; treatments = spontaneous, native microflora, and supplemented individually/combined with ***Lactobacillus plantarum* N8, *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* L, *Pediococcus pentosaceus* 12p**; medium contained **2% or 0.2% glucose and 4% NaCl**; **fermentations at 22 °C and 30 °C**.

Retrieved results:
- "**Sugar consumption, pH reduction and acid production were higher at 30 °C than at 22 °C.**"
- At **both** temperatures, spontaneous fermentation showed a **slower rate of pH reduction** than inoculated samples
- pH fall faster with 2% glucose than 0.2% glucose, but "minimum pH was in both case **lower than 3.0**"
- "**Maximum growth was reached between 2 and 5 days** of fermentation in all the samples assayed."
- After **30 days** with 2% glucose, "the survival of LAB was **nearly 5 log ufc/ml**. The **survival was higher at the lower temperature** assayed for both glucose concentrations."
- **Yeast behaviour differed by temperature:** at **22 °C** "a superficial layer with a creamy white colour could be observed… in the natural fermentation after 5 days and in the nonheating and inoculated fermentations after 10 days. The layer was… identified microscopically as **yeasts**." At **30 °C**, "in the nonheating and inoculated fermentations **presence of yeasts was not detected**."
- Sensory: "Organoleptic properties of peppers fermented with a **mixed culture of *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* and *Pediococcus pentosaceus*** were found **best** by a human panel. This sample has a relation **lactic acid/acetic acid of nearly 3**."

**Confidence: STRONG for the data** (peer-reviewed, full text retrieved, direct controlled comparison).
**Caveat: MODERATE overall** — *Food and Nutrition Sciences* is a SCIRP open-access title of lower tier than the other journals here; and the matrix is a glucose+NaCl solution over pepper, **not a high-salt mash**.

**Interpretation for the brief:** this is the closest retrievable answer to "what changes at 22 vs 30 °C". It shows a genuine **trade-off**: warmer = faster acidification; cooler = better LAB survival + yeast growth. It does **not** report colour, carotenoid or capsaicin data.

### 3b. Kádár, Păucean, Simon, Vodnar, Ranga, Rusu, Vișan, Man, Chiș & Drețcanu (2022) — 20 °C pure chili paste
"Dynamics of Bioactive Compounds during Spontaneous Fermentation of Paste Obtained from *Capsicum* ssp.—Stage towards a Product with Technological Application." *Plants* **11**(8):1080. DOI **10.3390/plants11081080**. PMCID **PMC9025496**. Full text retrieved: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9025496/

Design: six chili cultivars (Cherry, Bulgarian Chilli, Cayenne, Fatalii, Habanero, Carolina Reaper) from *C. annuum* and *C. chinense*; **anaerobic, spontaneous fermentation of pure chili paste for 21 days at 20 °C**.

Retrieved numbers:
- **Capsaicinoids:** "capsaicinoids are **very stable** compounds, as notable differences between unfermented and fermented samples could not be seen"
- **Ascorbic acid:** "Average loss of total ascorbic acid was **19.01%**"
- **Total phenolics:** **36.89–195.43 mg/100 g** fresh vs **35.60–180.40 mg/100 g** fermented; "Losses through fermentation were **not significant (p < 0.05)**"; Bulgarian cultivar highest at 195.43 ± 2.93 mg/100 g with losses of **7.69%**

**Confidence: STRONG** (peer-reviewed, PMC full text retrieved). Single temperature only (20 °C) — no comparison arm.

## 4. ★ HIGH SALT vs LOW SALT — COMMUNITY SELECTION AND SALT TOLERANCE

### 4a. ★★ Li, Dong, Zhao & Zhu (2020) — metagenomics of salt content in red pepper sauce
"Metagenomic insights into the changes in microbial community and antimicrobial resistance genes associated with different salt content of red pepper (*Capsicum annuum* L.) sauce." *Food Microbiology* **85**:103295. DOI **10.1016/j.fm.2019.103295**. Abstract verified via Europe PMC.

Retrieved verbatim findings:
- "While the family **Enterobacteriaceae dominated in high-salt (15–25%) samples**, **Lactobacillaceae quickly became the dominant population in place of Enterobacteriaceae after 24 days in 10% salt samples**."
- "Compared to **0.05 antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs) per cell number on average in 10% salt sample, 16.6 ARGs were present in high-salt samples**, wherein the bacterial hosts were major assigned to Enterobacteriaceae including genera ***Enterobacter*, *Citrobacter*, *Escherichia*, *Salmonella* and *Klebsiella*.**"
- "Multidrug resistance genes were the predominant ARG type."
- High-salt samples showed much higher abundance of histidine-kinase / osmotic-stress two-component systems that co-encoded ARGs.

**Confidence: STRONG for the abstract claims** (peer-reviewed, *Food Microbiology*, Elsevier; abstract retrieved and read in full).
**NOT VERIFIED:** the study's temperature, exact salt levels beyond the 10% and 15–25% buckets, strain-level composition (full text not accessible — ScienceDirect blocked).

**★ This directly answers Focus Question 4:** at **10% NaCl** LAB (Lactobacillaceae) *do* eventually take over — but only after **24 days**, and only at 10%, not at 15–25%. At **15–25% NaCl the community is dominated by Enterobacteriaceae**, i.e. high salt does **not** select for LAB in this matrix.

### 4b. ★ Aryee, Owusu-Kwarteng, Senwo & Alvarez (2022) — salt gradient 5–25% in habanero
"Characterizing fermented habanero pepper (*Capsicum chinense* L)." *Food Chemistry Advances* **1**:100137. DOI **10.1016/j.focha.2022.100137**. Abstract retrieved from FAO/AGRIS record: https://agris.fao.org/search/zh/records/675ac2880ce2cede71d067c4

Retrieved verbatim findings:
- "The effects of salt concentrations on microbial diversity, sensory, metabolite and aroma profiles of naturally fermented habanero pepper was investigated by adding **5 – 25% (w/w) of salt** to chopped habanero pepper and samples obtained on **day 0, 7, 14, 30 and 50**"
- "Lactic acid bacteria (LAB) increased from about **1.33 – 1.83 to 3.80 – 4.97 log CFU/mL** during fermentation while **pH decreased to 4.47 – 4.78**"
- "**The presence of Enterobacteriaceae throughout the fermentation period** reiterates the need to develop and implement starter cultures for fermenting habanero pepper."
- Metabolomics: **3206** metabolic features — **121** identified (confidence level 1), **1688** putatively identified (level 2), **1231** predicted with a chemical formula (level 3), **166** unidentified (level 4)
- "**Clustering within the fermented groups was salt concentration dependent, with low salt groups (0% and 5%) distinguished from high salt groups (10%, 15%, 20% and 25%).**"
- Sensory: "similar scores for the various fermented peppers"
- Volatiles: **18** compounds identified — **10 alcohols, 3 esters, 2 ketones, and an acid** — content varied with salt concentration

**Confidence: STRONG for the abstract** (peer-reviewed Elsevier journal; abstract retrieved).
**NOT VERIFIED:** fermentation temperature; which taxa; full text inaccessible.

**★ Note:** final LAB counts of only **3.80–4.97 log CFU/mL** and a pH floor of **4.47–4.78** (above the 4.6 botulism-control threshold for part of the range) means these high-salt pepper ferments were **microbiologically weak and did not reliably acidify**. This is consistent with Li 2020 and with Awad 1986's "10% salt inhibited most of the microflora".

### 4c. Etchells, Jones & Hoffman (1943) — salt-tolerance thresholds (SECONDARY ONLY — original NOT retrieved)
Quoted inside the literature review of Koh (2005), which I did retrieve:
> "They stated that the **salt concentration used, rather than the kind of vegetable, was the controlling factor on the character of the microbial flora and microbial activity**. In fermentation at **low salt content, 5 percent or less, large populations of acid forming and other bacteria occurred**. **At or above 15 percent salt, little or no growth of acid bacteria occurred.** Coliform bacteria developed rapidly in brines of **2.5 to 5 percent** of salt, with production of much gas… **Yeasts grew and fermented over a wide range of salt concentration and appeared not to be inhibited by the acidity** developed by lactic bacteria."

Full citation as given by Koh: Etchells, J. L., Jones, I. D., and Hoffman, M. A. 1943. "Brine preservation of vegetables." *Proc. Inst. Food Tech.* pp. 176–182.

**Confidence: WEAK–MODERATE** — this is a **secondary citation**; I did **not** retrieve the 1943 original. Directionally consistent with Li 2020 and Aryee 2022, but do not cite the 1943 numbers as primary without retrieving it.

### 4d. Koh (2005) literature review on LAB succession (secondary within a retrieved primary)
"**Leuconostoc mesenteroides** initiates the fermentation, then it disappears gradually to be replaced by **Lactobacillus plantarum**… *L. mesenteroides* is the most sensitive to decreasing pH and undissociated forms of lactic and acetic acids. *L. mesenteroides* initiates growth in vegetables more rapidly over a range of temperatures and salt concentrations than any other lactic acid bacteria." (Koh's review, citing Pederson 1971 — sauerkraut/vegetable model, **not pepper-specific**.)
**Confidence: WEAK** as pepper evidence — it is a general vegetable-fermentation model quoted second-hand.

### 4e. LAB genera actually documented IN pepper matrices (retrieved)
- Alberto 2013: *Lactobacillus plantarum*, *Leuconostoc mesenteroides*, *Pediococcus pentosaceus* — all three grow in pepper medium
- Di Cagno 2009: *Lactobacillus plantarum*, *Lactobacillus curvatus*, *Leuconostoc mesenteroides*, *Weissella confusa*
- Torán-Pereg 2023: *Lactobacillus plantarum* (commercial starter, pH 3.66)
- Kádár 2022: LAB and *Leuconostoc* enumerated (LAB to 6.2 × 10⁸ CFU/g at 21 d / 20 °C)
- Li 2020: Lactobacillaceae dominant at 10% salt after 24 days
- Janiszewska-Turak 2022: *Levilactobacillus brevis*, *Limosilactobacillus fermentum*, *Lactoplantibacillus plantarum*
- **McIlhenny finished Tabasco sauce: LAB <10 cfu/g** (company spec sheet) — i.e. no live LAB survive into the retail product

**Salt-tolerance limit for LAB in pepper mash — status:** No retrieved source states a single numeric NaCl limit for LAB *in pepper mash*. The convergent evidence is: LAB dominate/acidify at ≤5% and at 10% (after a lag of ~24 days); at 15–25% NaCl they do not dominate and Enterobacteriaceae persist. **Treat "the LAB salt-tolerance limit in pepper mash is X%" as NOT VERIFIED.**

## 5. COLOUR / CAROTENOID / CAPSAICINOID CHANGES

**Retrieved facts:**
- **Capsaicinoids are robust.** Koh 2005: fermentation did not affect capsaicinoid concentration; capsaicin ~0.30–0.32 mg/g stable across 24 months; dihydrocapsaicin 0.12 → 0.09–0.10 mg/g. Kádár 2022: "capsaicinoids are very stable compounds, as notable differences between unfermented and fermented samples could not be seen" (21 d, 20 °C). **Consistent across two independent studies.**
- **Colour change over long barrel aging is small.** Watts 2018 (18 months): "There were minor changes in the colour of the mash."
- **Colour change in short lab ferments is measurable and process-dependent.** Torán-Pereg 2023 (15 d, 21–24 °C): ΔE* 11.92 (spontaneous) vs 5.66 (inoculated *L. plantarum*); a* moved from −7.87 (raw green) to +2.45 / +1.97; limonene 19.65% vs 7.21%.
- **Salt and vessel affect colour.** Awad 1986: the **8% salt / oak barrel** mash showed **less colour change** than other treatments; higher salt retarded pectin degradation. ΔE was measured at 6/8/10% over 2 years (tables present; individual ΔE values not text-extractable from the OCR).
- **Pigment class matters.** Janiszewska-Turak 2022: "significant degradation of chlorophylls into pheophytins" after fermentation; red bell peppers changed least, green changed most.
- **Carotenoids are affected by temperature — but the supporting citations were NOT retrieved.** Awad 1986's literature review states carotenoids such as **capsanthin** "were also affected by the light and temperature during the fermentation process of vegetable products", citing Dougherty & Nelson 1974, MacKinney & Little 1962, and Michael & Eskin 1979 — **none of which I retrieved. Do not cite those as verified.**
- **Ascorbic acid, not carotenoid, is the vitamin most degraded.** Kádár 2022 (20 °C, 21 d): total ascorbic acid loss **19.01%**; phenolics not significantly lost.

**★ Bottom line for Question 5:** **No retrieved study quantifies carotenoid or browning change as a function of pepper-mash fermentation temperature.** It is a genuine evidence gap. The nearest retrievable statements are (i) Awad 1986's ambient/warehouse design with 8%-salt-oak showing least colour change, (ii) Watts 2018's "minor colour changes" over 18 months, and (iii) Awad's second-hand claim that capsanthin is light/temperature sensitive. **Mark as a research gap / NOT VERIFIED.**

## 7. GUIDANCE FOR DOWNSTREAM USE

**Safe to state with citation:**
- Oak barrels, up to 3 years, salt-sealed tops, 28-day vinegar blend, ~73,000 barrels — McIlhenny/Tabasco 2019.
- Original 1870 process = ~6 weeks, vinegar+salt, bisulphate of lime added to *prevent* fermentation — US Patent 107,701.
- Finished Tabasco sauce: pH 2.70–3.10, salt 1.40–2.20%, acetic acid 8.10–8.90%, LAB <10 cfu/g — McIlhenny spec sheet.
- 8% salt Tabasco mash: pH 4.98 → 4.7 (salted) → 3.7–3.9 by 1 month; TA 0.58% → ~1.6% by 1 month → 1.5% at 24 months; capsaicin unchanged — Koh 2005.
- 6/8/10% salt comparison; 6% most acid, 10% inhibited flora; summer heat *reduced* acidity; 8%+oak least colour change; barrels at **ambient temperature** — Awad 1986.
- 22 °C vs 30 °C at 4% NaCl: 30 °C faster acidification, 22 °C better LAB survival and yeast growth — Alberto 2013.
- 20 °C / 21 d pure chili paste: capsaicinoids stable, ascorbic acid −19.01%, lactic acid 0.02% → 1.13%, LAB to 6.2 × 10⁸ CFU/g — Kádár 2022.
- Enterobacteriaceae dominate at 15–25% salt; Lactobacillaceae take over after 24 days at 10% salt — Li 2020.
- Habanero at 5–25% salt: LAB only reach 3.80–4.97 log CFU/mL, pH only falls to 4.47–4.78, Enterobacteriaceae persist throughout — Aryee 2022.

**Must NOT be stated as fact:**
- Any specific Tabasco barrel-warehouse temperature.
- That McIlhenny uses 8–10% salt (company says "a small amount").
- That high salt (8–10%) selects for LAB in pepper mash — the retrieved evidence points the other way.
- Any numeric LAB salt-tolerance ceiling in pepper mash.
- Any quantitative carotenoid/browning-vs-temperature relationship for pepper mash.
